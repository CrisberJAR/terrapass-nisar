    04 · Synthetic Aperture Radar physics

    Five rules. Zero black boxes.

    Each detection is a hypothesis backed by physics and a 5-part confidence score (magnitude, persistence, coherence, rule agreement, baseline depth). Change is measured with a robust z-score: median and MAD of at least four previous passes.

    Polarimetric decision space · selected cell

    1 · Crustal deformation

    GUNW unwrapped phase → line-of-sight displacement d = −λΔφ / 4π > 8 mm with coherence γ > 0.25.

    2 · Open-water flooding

    ΔHH < −4.5 dB and ΔHV < −4.0 dB: calm water acts as a mirror (specular reflection).

    3 · Flooding under canopy

    ΔHH > +2.5 dB with stable HV: trunk–water double bounce, invisible to optical satellites.

    4 · Vegetation loss

    ΔHV < −3.0 dB for ≥ 3 passes: less volume scattering from the canopy.

    5 · Agricultural activity

    ΔHH < −3.0 dB over 1–3 passes then recovery: rice paddy flooding, ploughing.

        Declared limits

        • Real-data change maps use browse quicklooks (relative dB, ~150 m), not calibrated γ⁰ from HDF5 — the pipeline is ready to swap in full-resolution GCOV.
        • Georeferencing uses the granule footprint bounding box; expect sub-cell offsets.
        • Robust baseline needs ≥ 4 previous passes (48 days); earlier passes are not classified.
        • Every output is a hypothesis with a confidence breakdown, never a verdict.

        Glossary

        SAR
        Synthetic Aperture Radar: an active sensor that images day and night, through clouds.
        HH / HV
        Transmit-receive polarisations. HH ↔ surface & double bounce; HV ↔ volume (vegetation).
        Interferogram (GUNW)
        Phase difference of two passes: each colour cycle is a contour of ground motion.
        Coherence γ
        How similar two passes are (0–1). Low γ = the surface was disturbed.
